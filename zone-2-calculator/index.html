Zone 2 heart rate calculator

By the RunZone team · Updated October 2026

Enter your age (and resting heart rate if you know it) to get your Zone 2 heart rate range, plus all five zones.

How this calculator works

If you add your resting heart rate, it uses the Karvonen method: Zone 2 = resting HR + 60–70% of your heart rate reserve (max HR minus resting HR). This fits fitter runners better, because a low resting heart rate shifts every zone.

Without a resting heart rate, it uses 60–70% of max heart rate, with max estimated as 220 minus your age. That estimate can be off by 10–15 beats either way, so treat it as a starting point.

How to use your Zone 2 range

  • Run about 80% of your weekly miles in Zone 2. That's how most elite endurance athletes train.
  • Expect to slow down a lot at first, often 1–2 minutes per mile slower than your usual pace. It speeds up over a few weeks.
  • Watch for heart rate drift. Heat, hills and bad sleep push your heart rate up at the same pace, so slow down to stay in range.
  • No watch? Use the talk test. Full sentences means you're in Zone 2.

FAQ

What heart rate is Zone 2?

For most people, Zone 2 is roughly 60–70% of heart rate reserve (the Karvonen method) or about 60–70% of max heart rate if you don't know your resting heart rate. For a 30-year-old with a resting heart rate of 60, that's about 138–151 bpm.

Is 220 minus age accurate?

It's a rough estimate. Real max heart rate can be 10–15 bpm higher or lower. Adding your resting heart rate (Karvonen) makes the zones fit you better, and a lab test or a recent all-out effort is the most accurate.

How do I know I'm in Zone 2 without a heart rate monitor?

Use the talk test: you should be able to speak in full sentences, breathing only slightly harder than walking. If you can only get a few words out, slow down.
