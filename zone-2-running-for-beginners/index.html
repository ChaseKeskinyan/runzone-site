Zone 2 running for beginners: a 4-week plan

By the RunZone team · Updated October 2026

Zone 2 running for beginners means running slow enough to talk, 3 times a week, and walking whenever your heart rate climbs too high. Follow the 4-week plan below and most beginners can run 30 minutes in Zone 2 by the end of it.

Step 1: Find your Zone 2

Use the free Zone 2 calculator. Add your resting heart rate if you know it; it makes the range fit you better. No watch? Use the talk test: full sentences, breathing a little harder than walking.

Step 2: Expect it to feel too easy

Most beginners are surprised how slow Zone 2 is. You may need to walk up hills or take walk breaks to stay in range. That's not failure; it's exactly how the aerobic base gets built. Here's why it feels so slow.

The 4-week plan

Week3 sessions a week (keep heart rate in Zone 2)
120 min: walk 5, then alternate 1 min easy run / 2 min walk
225 min: walk 5, then alternate 2 min run / 2 min walk
325–30 min: walk 5, then alternate 4 min run / 1 min walk
430 min: walk 5, then run as much as you can while staying in Zone 2

Take at least one rest day between sessions. If a week feels hard, repeat it. There's no prize for rushing.

4 beginner mistakes

  1. Running to pace instead of effort. Ignore pace for the first month.
  2. Doing too much too soon. Your lungs adapt faster than your legs, so build slowly.
  3. Skipping rest days. Fitness is built while you recover.
  4. Quitting in week 2. It's the hardest week. It gets easier after that.

After week 4

Keep 3–4 Zone 2 runs a week and add 5 minutes to one run each week. After 6–8 weeks, you can add one harder session, like short strides or intervals, if you want to get faster.

FAQ

How slow should Zone 2 feel for a beginner?

Slow enough to speak in full sentences. Many beginners need to mix walking and running at first to keep their heart rate in Zone 2, and that's normal.

Can I do Zone 2 if I've never run before?

Yes. Start with brisk walking and short run intervals. As your aerobic base grows, you'll run more of each session without leaving the zone.

Do I need a heart rate monitor?

It helps, but the talk test works: if you can talk comfortably, you're close to Zone 2.
