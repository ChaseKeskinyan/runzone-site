Best Nike Run Club alternatives in 2026

By the RunZone team · Updated November 2026

The best Nike Run Club alternative depends on what you want that NRC doesn't give you: a plan that adapts to you (Runna or Garmin Coach), training built around heart rate zones (RunZone), or a bigger social feed (Strava). If you mostly love NRC's free audio Guided Runs, nothing else matches them for $0, so you may only need to add a second tool rather than switch.

Disclosure: RunZone makes this site and is on this list. Prices are US list prices as checked in November 2026 and can change; confirm on each app's own pricing page.

What Nike Run Club does well

NRC is free, with no paid tier. It has a large library of audio Guided Runs led by Nike coaches and athletes, coached training plans from a first run up to the marathon, GPS tracking, and Apple Watch support. For a lot of runners that's plenty, and it's a fair benchmark for every app below.

Why people look for an NRC alternative

  • Plans that adapt: NRC's plans are largely fixed. If you miss a week or improve quickly, the plan doesn't rebuild itself around you.
  • Heart rate training: some runners want every easy run kept in a specific heart rate zone, such as Zone 2, rather than going by feel.
  • Race-specific detail: goal-time plans with set paces and structured workouts.
  • Community and analysis: segments, clubs and deeper stats live elsewhere.

1RunZone: best for Zone 2 heart rate training

RunZone is an iPhone app that builds your plan around Zone 2: 5K to marathon, plus weight loss and general fitness plans. While the app is open it coaches you live with voice cues, telling you to ease off or pick it up based on your heart rate from Apple Watch (via Apple Health), or on easy-pace guidance if you run with just your phone. Its main progress view charts your pace at the same heart rate, so you can see easy runs getting faster. It also has Run Age (a fitness age from your VO2 max), AI Form Check, an AI coach and XP ranks. Trade-offs: iPhone only, and no free tier.

2Runna: best for race plans with set paces

Runna builds plans around a goal race and time, with structured intervals, tempo runs and paces that update as you train. It's the closest thing to a personal coach for a specific race. The free version covers week 1 of a plan; after that you need the subscription. Runna is also available as a yearly bundle with Strava ($149.99/year).

3Garmin Coach: best free option if you own a Garmin

If you already have a compatible Garmin watch, Garmin Coach gives you adaptive race plans at no extra cost, with workouts sent to your wrist. It's a good fit if you'd rather leave your phone at home, but it doesn't help if you don't own a Garmin.

4Strava: best for tracking and community

Strava is where many runners log every run, follow friends and chase segments. The free version records and shares activities; the subscription adds deeper analysis and route tools. It isn't a coaching app on its own, so many runners pair it with something else.

5adidas Running: best free like-for-like swap

adidas Running (formerly Runtastic) is free for GPS tracking, stats, challenges and community, works with Apple Watch, and offers training plans and voice coaching with an optional Premium upgrade. If you just want a different free tracker, it's the most direct swap.

How to choose

  1. Keep NRC if you mainly run for the Guided Runs and don't need a plan to adapt.
  2. Add heart rate coaching if your easy runs keep turning into hard ones. Find your range with the Zone 2 calculator first, then decide whether you want an app to coach it.
  3. Pick a race planner if you have a date and a goal time.
  4. Use your watch's own coaching if you already own a Garmin.

FAQ

Is Nike Run Club still free?

Yes. As of November 2026 Nike Run Club has no paid tier. Guided Runs, training plans and run tracking are free with a Nike account.

What is the best free alternative to Nike Run Club?

For Garmin owners, Garmin Coach. For everyone else, adidas Running and the free version of Strava are the closest like-for-like swaps for tracking, though neither replaces NRC's Guided Runs.

Which Nike Run Club alternative is best for Zone 2 training?

An app that coaches by heart rate zone during the run. RunZone is built around Zone 2 and gives live voice cues from your Apple Watch heart rate. It is paid ($39.99/year) and iPhone only.
