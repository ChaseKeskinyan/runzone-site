Zone 2 heart rate for a 25-year-old

By the RunZone team · Updated October 2026

For a 25-year-old, Zone 2 heart rate is roughly 117–136 bpm, based on an estimated max heart rate of 195 bpm. If you know your resting heart rate, the Karvonen table below gives a more personal range.

Two common max heart rate estimates: 220 minus age, and the Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age). Real max heart rate can differ from both by 10–15 bpm.

Zone 2 at age 25 by resting heart rate

The Karvonen method uses your heart rate reserve (max minus resting), so fitter people with lower resting heart rates get a range that fits them better. Zone 2 = resting + 60–70% of reserve.

Zone 2 training at 25

In your 20s, recovery is fast, so the main risk is running every run too hard. Most runners this age can handle 3–5 Zone 2 runs a week plus one harder session.

  • Talk test: full sentences means you're in Zone 2.
  • Expect to slow down: many runners need to run 1–2 minutes per mile slower at first.
  • Track progress: your pace at the same heart rate should improve over 4–8 weeks. How long Zone 2 takes.

General fitness information, not medical advice. If you have a heart condition or take medication that affects heart rate, ask your doctor before training by heart rate.

FAQ

What is Zone 2 heart rate for a 25-year-old?

Using the common 220-minus-age estimate, max heart rate is about 195 bpm, so Zone 2 (60–70% of max) is about 117–136 bpm. Adding your resting heart rate gives a more personal range.

Is 136 bpm too high for Zone 2 at 25?

Not necessarily. 136 bpm is the top of the estimated range for age 25. Max heart rate varies by 10–15 bpm between people, so use the talk test as a check: if you can speak in full sentences, you're close to Zone 2.

How do I find my exact Zone 2?

Add your resting heart rate to the Zone 2 calculator (Karvonen method), or get a lab test. The talk test is a good free cross-check.
