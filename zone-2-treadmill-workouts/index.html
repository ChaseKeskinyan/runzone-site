5 Zone 2 treadmill workouts

By the RunZone team · Updated October 2026

The treadmill is one of the easiest places to train in Zone 2, because you control the speed and incline exactly. These 5 workouts keep you in the zone without getting bored.

Before you start

  • Know your range: get it from the Zone 2 calculator.
  • Use a fan. Indoor heat pushes heart rate up at the same speed.
  • Go by heart rate, not the display. If your heart rate climbs, slow the belt or lower the incline.

1. The incline walk (beginner)

40 minutes at a brisk walk with 6–12% incline. Adjust incline to stay in Zone 2. Great for beginners and for lifters who want cardio without pounding their legs.

2. The run-walk ladder (beginner)

Walk 5 minutes, then alternate 3 minutes easy jog and 1 minute walk for 30 minutes. Lengthen the jog by 1 minute each week.

3. The steady 40 (intermediate)

5-minute walk, 30 minutes at a steady easy jog at 1% incline, 5-minute walk. Note your average speed at the same heart rate each week; it should creep up.

4. The rolling hills (intermediate)

45 minutes at an easy jog, switching between 1% and 4% incline every 5 minutes. Slow the belt slightly on the hills so your heart rate stays in range.

5. The long indoor run (advanced)

60–75 minutes in Zone 2, changing speed by 0.1–0.2 mph every 10 minutes to keep heart rate steady as you warm up and drift. Bring water.

How to track progress

Pick one workout, like the steady 40, and repeat it every 2 weeks. If you're running faster at the same heart rate, your aerobic base is growing.

FAQ

Is treadmill Zone 2 the same as outdoors?

Close. Many runners find their heart rate a bit higher indoors because there's less airflow, so use a fan and go by heart rate, not the speed on the display.

Should I use an incline for Zone 2?

A 1% incline roughly mimics outdoor effort for many runners. Walking at a steeper incline is also a great way to stay in Zone 2 if running pushes you too high.

How long should a treadmill Zone 2 session be?

30–45 minutes is a good range for most runners. Longer sessions work too, but break them up with incline or speed changes to stay engaged.
