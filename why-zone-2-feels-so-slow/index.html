Why Zone 2 running feels so slow

By the RunZone team · Updated October 2026

If Zone 2 feels embarrassingly slow, that's normal. It usually means your aerobic base is underdeveloped, which is exactly what Zone 2 fixes. For most runners, the pace starts improving within 4–8 weeks.

5 reasons Zone 2 feels so slow

  1. You've been training in the gray zone. If every run was medium-hard, your body never built the low-intensity engine Zone 2 uses.
  2. Your aerobic base is small. Your heart rate climbs quickly because your body isn't yet efficient at delivering oxygen. This improves with consistent easy miles.
  3. Conditions push your heart rate up. Heat, humidity, hills, poor sleep and caffeine all raise heart rate at the same pace.
  4. Your zones might be off. 220-minus-age can be wrong by 10–15 beats. Add your resting heart rate in the calculator for a better range.
  5. Ego. Most runners are used to judging a run by pace. Zone 2 asks you to judge it by effort.

What's normal

  • Running 1–2+ minutes per mile slower than your usual pace in the first weeks.
  • Walking up hills to stay in range.
  • Your heart rate drifting up late in a run, especially when it's warm.

When it gets faster

With 3+ easy runs a week, many runners see their Zone 2 pace drop within 1–2 months, then keep improving for months. The trick is tracking it: compare your average pace at the same heart rate week by week, not your fastest run.

FAQ

How long until Zone 2 gets faster?

Many runners notice their Zone 2 pace improving within 4–8 weeks of consistent easy running, 3 or more times a week. Gains keep coming for months.

Is it OK to walk during Zone 2 runs?

Yes. Walking to keep your heart rate in range is normal early on, especially on hills or in heat.

Why is my heart rate high even at a slow pace?

Heat, humidity, poor sleep, stress, caffeine and dehydration all raise heart rate. A low aerobic base does too, which is exactly what Zone 2 training improves.
