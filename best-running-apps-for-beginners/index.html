What a beginner actually needs from an app

Most new runners don't quit because of the wrong shoes. They quit because they run too fast, every run hurts, and progress feels invisible. A good beginner app fixes that in three ways:

  1. Tells you how hard to go, by voice, while you run. Slower than you think is usually right.
  2. Gives you a plan so you know what to do on Tuesday without deciding.
  3. Shows progress beyond pace: consistency, time on feet, or the same pace at a lower heart rate.

1RunZone: best for easy, coached Zone 2 running

RunZone builds a weekly plan around easy Zone 2 running, for goals from a first 5K to a marathon, or for weight loss and general fitness. While the app is open it coaches you with live voice cues to slow down or speed up, using heart rate from Apple Watch via Apple Health, or pace-only coaching if you just have your phone. A chart shows your pace getting faster at the same heart rate, and Run Age turns your VO2 max into a fitness age. iPhone only, no free tier.

2Nike Run Club: best free all-rounder

Free, with a big library of audio-guided runs that talk you through your first runs and help motivation. It has basic training plans and works with Apple Watch and Wear OS. It doesn't build your training around heart rate zones.

3NHS Couch to 5K: best for absolute beginners

The UK health service's free program takes you from no running to 30 minutes over about 9 weeks with run/walk intervals, a countdown timer and audio cues over your own music. You can repeat weeks if needed. App store availability varies by country; similar Couch to 5K apps exist if it isn't in yours.

4Strava: best for logging and community

Free Strava records runs with GPS and gives you a social feed, kudos and clubs, which helps some people stay consistent. It doesn't coach you or build a plan. The paid subscription adds features like heart rate zone analysis and goals. Strava alternatives compares more options.

5Runna: best for a race goal

Runna builds structured plans for 5K to marathon goals, with intervals and tempo runs and a 7-day free trial when you sign up directly. It's owned by Strava, with a Strava + Runna bundle for $149.99/year. It's the priciest option here and leans more on pace-based workouts than easy heart rate running.

6Apple Workout app: best no-download option

If you have an Apple Watch, the built-in Workout app is free and shows heart rate zones, pace and distance during your run. No plan or coaching, but it's a solid tracker to start with.

How to choose

  • Total beginner, want free: Couch to 5K, then Nike Run Club.
  • Want running to feel easier and stick: a Zone 2 app like RunZone. Find your range first with the Zone 2 calculator.
  • Have a race date: Runna or RunZone's race plans.
  • Just want to log runs with friends: Strava.

Whichever you pick, run slowly enough to talk for most runs, 3 times a week, for a month. That habit matters more than the app. Our 4-week Zone 2 plan for beginners is a good place to start.

FAQ

What is the best free running app for beginners?

Nike Run Club and Couch to 5K apps are the strongest free picks. Both give you audio-guided runs and a simple structure without paying anything.

Do I need a smartwatch to use a running app?

No. All the apps here track runs with your phone's GPS. A watch with heart rate adds accuracy for heart-rate-based training like Zone 2.

Is a paid running app worth it for a beginner?

Only if you want a plan that adapts to you, live coaching or heart rate guidance. Many beginners start free and upgrade once running is a habit.
