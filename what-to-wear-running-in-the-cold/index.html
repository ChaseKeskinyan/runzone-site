What to wear running in the cold (by temperature)

By the RunZone team · Updated November 2026

Dress for running in the cold as if it's about 15–20°F (8–10°C) warmer than the thermometer says, because your body heats up fast once you're moving. You should feel slightly cold for the first 5–10 minutes; if you're warm standing at the door, you'll overheat by the first mile.

The basic rule: layers, not bulk

Cold-weather running kit works in three layers, and you add them as the temperature drops:

  • Base layer: a close-fitting top made of synthetic fabric or merino wool that pulls sweat away from your skin. Skip cotton. It stays wet and makes you colder.
  • Mid layer: a thermal or fleece-lined top for proper cold days. Many runners only need this below about 25°F (-4°C).
  • Outer layer: a light wind-resistant jacket or vest. Wind is what makes cold runs miserable, so blocking it matters more than thickness.

Your hands, ears and neck lose heat quickly, so small accessories make a big difference for very little weight.

What to wear at each temperature

Use this as a starting point. Add a layer if it's windy, wet or you run slowly, and take one away if you run hard or tend to run hot.

Hands, head and feet

  • Gloves: thin running gloves cover most days. Below about 20°F (-7°C), mittens keep fingers warmer because they share heat. Cheap gloves are fine to tuck in a pocket once you warm up.
  • Head and ears: a headband is enough around freezing; a beanie for colder runs. A neck gaiter can be pulled over your mouth and nose to warm the air on very cold days.
  • Feet: wool-blend running socks stay warm when damp. Your normal running shoes are fine for most cold days; on snow or ice, trail shoes or slip-on traction devices give much better grip.

Adjust for wind, rain and darkness

  1. Check the "feels like" temperature, not just the air temperature. A 30°F day with strong wind can feel closer to 20°F.
  2. Start into the wind when you can, so it's at your back on the way home when you're sweaty.
  3. Wet and cold is the worst mix. In cold rain, a water-resistant shell over a thin base layer usually beats more insulation.
  4. Be visible. Winter runs often happen in the dark, so wear bright or reflective kit and a small light.

Running easy in the cold

Cold days are a good fit for Zone 2 running, but there are two traps. Overdressing pushes your heart rate up as you overheat, and running fast just to stay warm turns an easy run into a hard one. Dress so you can hold an easy, conversational effort comfortably, and check you're in your range with the Zone 2 calculator. If heavy layers make it hard to stay easy, a Zone 2 treadmill workout is a fine swap.

A few more habits help:

  • Warm up for a few minutes indoors (brisk walking, leg swings) so you leave the house already moving.
  • Do shorter loops near home on very cold days so you can cut the run short if you need to.
  • Change out of damp clothes straight after your run. You cool down fast once you stop.
  • Wrist heart rate readings can be less steady in the cold. A snug watch fit under your sleeve helps.

How cold is too cold?

Most healthy runners can run safely well below freezing with the right kit. The bigger risk is exposed skin in wind: once the wind chill drops to around -20°F (-28°C), frostbite can develop on exposed skin in about 30 minutes. On days like that, cover every bit of skin or run indoors. Watch for numb, white or waxy patches on your fingers, ears, nose or cheeks, and get warm right away if you notice them.

This is general fitness information, not medical advice. If cold air makes your breathing difficult or you have a heart or lung condition, check with your doctor before running in the cold.

FAQ

What should I wear running in 40 degree weather?

At 40°F (about 4°C), most runners are comfortable in a long-sleeve technical top with shorts or light tights. Add light gloves and a headband if it's windy or you tend to feel the cold.

How cold is too cold to run outside?

Most healthy runners can run well below freezing with the right layers. The main danger is exposed skin in strong wind. When the wind chill is around -20°F (-28°C) or lower, frostbite can develop in about 30 minutes, so cover all skin or run indoors.

Should I wear shorts running in the cold?

Many runners wear shorts down to about 40°F (4°C) because legs generate a lot of heat. Below that, tights are usually more comfortable, and thermal tights help below about 20°F (-7°C).
