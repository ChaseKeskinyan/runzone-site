Things nobody tells you when you start running

By the RunZone team · Updated October 2026

Starting running is harder than it looks, and most of the reasons have nothing to do with fitness. Here's what nobody tells you in the first month.

  1. You're probably running too fast. Most beginners start at a pace they can't sustain. If you can't talk, slow down, or walk.
  2. Your lungs adapt faster than your legs. Breathing gets easier in weeks; bones, tendons and joints take months. That's why doing too much too soon causes shin splints.
  3. Walk breaks are real training. Run 2 minutes, walk 1 is a legitimate method, not cheating.
  4. The first 10 minutes are always the worst. Even experienced runners feel stiff early on. It settles.
  5. Side stitches are common and tend to fade as you get fitter. Slow down and breathe out fully.
  6. You'll still have bad runs when you're fit. Sleep, stress and weather matter more than you'd think.
  7. Add mileage slowly, about 10% a week at most.
  8. Shoes matter more than gear. Get a comfortable pair with room at the toe and replace them every 300–500 miles.
  9. Consistency beats intensity. Three easy runs a week for two months beats one hard run whenever you feel like it.
  10. It clicks. Somewhere around weeks 6–8, it starts to feel different. Most people quit right before that.
